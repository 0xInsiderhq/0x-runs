This is a test of your ceiling. Make the most photorealistic video you are capable of: a cyberpunk city at night, entirely in code, from this one prompt. No stock footage, no downloaded images, textures or 3D models, no image or video generators. You may install software from the package sources you can reach (for example Blender for Python), but every model, material, light and sound must be built by your own code.

Scene: your call. Night, rain, wet streets reflecting neon, haze and steam, signs with text you invent. No real brands, logos, films, games or existing characters. A human close-up is the hardest thing to make look real. Include people only if they hold up at full resolution.

Realism targets:
- physically based lighting and materials, real reflections on wet surfaces
- a moving camera with believable lens behaviour: depth of field, motion blur, bloom, slight distortion, subtle grain
- every frame truly rendered, no held, repeated or duplicated frames, no slow motion made by repeating frames

Length: exactly 15 seconds at 24 fps, 360 frames. Minimum 1280x720.

There is no time limit. Quality comes first. Do not stop at good enough: keep iterating until you cannot make it look more real. Before the final render, time one test frame at final settings so the render cannot die halfway, then push resolution and samples as high as you can actually finish. Tell me why you chose the settings you chose.

Sound, also made in code. Music is required: an original track of your choice, no existing melodies, that carries the film and hits the key visual moments. Mix it with rain, city ambience and distant traffic.

Deliverable: one MP4, exactly 15.0 s, H.264 + AAC, about -16 LUFS, checked with ffprobe. Also save 3 full-resolution stills of your best frames as PNG.

Work like a one-person VFX studio: render small previews, look at your own frames, fix what looks fake, render again. Write down the clock time (UTC) when you start and when you finish.

At the end report: total time, render engine and settings, render time per frame, how many frames you rendered, what music you chose and why, what you fixed after looking at previews, which frame looks most real and which looks least real, and what still gives it away as CG.
